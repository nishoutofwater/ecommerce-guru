# Eisenhower Matrix

**Origin:** Attributed to Dwight D. Eisenhower, popularised by Stephen Covey (1989).

## Core idea
Sort tasks by urgency and importance to decide what to do, plan, delegate or drop.

|  | **Urgent** | **Not urgent** |
|---|---|---|
| **Important** | **Do now**: site down, payment failing, stockout on hero SKU, compliance issue, broken checkout | **Schedule**: SEO foundations, email flows, brand positioning, retention programme, CRO tests |
| **Not important** | **Delegate/automate**: routine customer emails, order status queries, minor listing edits, reporting pulls | **Drop**: vanity metrics, redesigns without hypotheses, chasing every new channel |

## How this skill uses it
Every plan or audit produced by this skill ends with an Eisenhower sort of the recommended actions, so output is a prioritised backlog, not a wish list.

Scoring for "important" in e-com: does it move revenue, margin, conversion, retention, or reduce risk (legal, payment, platform ban)?

## When to use
- Turning an audit into a backlog
- Weekly planning for a small team or solo founder
- Deciding what to automate or outsource

## Diagnostic questions
1. What breaks revenue today if ignored? (Do now.)
2. What compounds over months? (Schedule, and protect the time.)
3. What could a tool, VA or flow handle? (Delegate.)
4. What are we doing because a competitor does it? (Often drop.)

## Common misuse
- Living in the urgent quadrants. Most e-com growth (SEO, retention, brand) sits in Important, Not urgent.
