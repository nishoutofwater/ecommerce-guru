# Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB)

**Origin:** Icek Ajzen (1985, 1991). One of the most tested models of intention and behaviour in social psychology.

## Core idea
A person's intention to act (buy, subscribe, sign up) is driven by three beliefs. Intention plus actual ability predicts behaviour.

| Component | Question the shopper asks | E-com levers |
|---|---|---|
| **Attitude** | "Is this product good for me?" | Benefit-led copy, demos, before/after, clear outcome, comparison tables |
| **Subjective norms** | "Do people like me buy this? Would they approve?" | Reviews, UGC, ratings count, "X bought this week", expert or community endorsement, press logos |
| **Perceived behavioural control** | "Can I do this easily and safely?" | Shipping cost and time upfront, returns policy, guarantees, payment options, size guides, simple checkout, support access |

## When to use
- Diagnosing why a product page or landing page converts poorly
- Writing PDP (product detail page) copy and structure
- Deciding what trust elements to add
- Researching objections before a launch

## Diagnostic questions
1. Attitude: can a shopper state the main benefit within 5 seconds of landing?
2. Norms: is there social proof from people who resemble the target segment (not generic praise)?
3. Control: which friction points are visible before checkout (hidden costs, unclear delivery, account creation)?
4. Which of the three is weakest? Fix the weakest first.

## E-com application
- Map each PDP section to one of the three components. A page heavy on attitude but light on control (common) stalls at checkout.
- Abandoned carts usually signal a **control** problem (cost surprise, payment, trust), not an attitude problem.
- New categories or unfamiliar products need more **norms** content.
- Post-purchase surveys can measure all three ("What almost stopped you buying?").

## Common misuse
- Treating TPB as copy only. Control is often operational (shipping speed, returns), not messaging.
- Using fake or generic social proof. It erodes norms and can breach consumer protection rules.

## Pairs well with
STP (whose attitudes and norms?), Zeigarnik (reduce drop-off once intention exists), Rule of 7 (building attitude over repeated exposure), RACE Convert stage.
