# Racecar Growth Framework

**Origin:** Lenny Rachitsky with Dan Hockenmaier, Lenny's Newsletter (2022). This file is a summary in our own words for e-com use. Read the original for the full detail, examples and links: https://www.lennysnewsletter.com

## Core idea
Growth is a car with six parts. Each has a different job and a different moment in a company's life.

| Part | What it is | E-com translation |
|---|---|---|
| **Growth engine** | The self-sustaining loop that drives most long-term growth, where output can be reinvested | One or two of: **SEO** (content drives search traffic, which drives sales, which funds more content), **Paid** (ads drive sales, revenue funds more ads), **Sales** (B2B/wholesale reps), **Virality** (word of mouth, referrals, sharing, UGC) |
| **Kickstarts** | Unscalable tactics to get the first customers | Friends and network, targeted outreach (DMs, emails), going where the audience gathers (forums, communities, events), influencers, press, viral content, physical placement (pop-ups, samples, retail), hosting events, being early on a new platform |
| **Turbo boosts** | One-off events that spike growth temporarily | Launch PR, creator mentions, campaigns, drops, sponsorships, viral mini-products or tools, features on high-traffic platforms, podcasts |
| **Lubricants** | Optimisations that make the engine run efficiently | Conversion rate, retention and repeat rate, brand awareness, activation (first-use success), pricing |
| **Mid-stage accelerants** | Re-accelerate growth once at scale | Channel partners (marketplaces, retailers, affiliates), geographic expansion, category expansion |
| **Fuel** | The input the engine runs on | **Content** fuels SEO, **customers** fuel virality, **capital** fuels paid and sales |

## Sequencing (the useful part)
1. Early: focus on **kickstarts** and a few turbo boosts until one growth engine works.
2. Engine working: invest in **lubricants**, plus occasional turbo boosts.
3. At meaningful scale: try one or two **mid-stage accelerants**.
4. Before the main engine plateaus: start a **second engine** while still lubricating the first.

## E-com specifics
- Most DTC stores run on **paid** first, then add **SEO** and **virality** (referrals, UGC) to reduce CAC dependence.
- Categories with ad restrictions (regulated products, some supplements, finance, crypto) often cannot rely on paid. SEO, virality, affiliates and communities become the realistic engines. Check platform policies first.
- Lubricants in e-com are mostly CRO and retention: PDP, checkout, email/SMS flows, subscriptions, loyalty.
- Fuel check: SEO without a content pipeline stalls; paid without margin to reinvest stalls.

## Diagnostic questions
1. What is our growth engine today? (If "nothing drives most growth", we are still in kickstart mode.)
2. Which engine fits our category, margin and channel restrictions?
3. Is the fuel supply (content, customers, capital) sufficient for that engine?
4. Which lubricant has the biggest gap (conversion, retention, AOV/price)?
5. Are we spending on turbo boosts expecting lasting growth?

## Common misuse
- Treating turbo boosts as an engine.
- Scaling paid before conversion and retention lubricants are in place.
- Starting a second engine before the first works.
