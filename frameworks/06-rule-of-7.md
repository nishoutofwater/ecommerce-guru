# The Rule of 7

**Origin:** Marketing heuristic often attributed to 1930s film studio advertising. The idea that a prospect needs around seven exposures before acting.

**Evidence note:** There is no robust study behind the number seven. Frequency research (effective frequency, Krugman's three-exposure theory) supports the general point that repeated, varied exposure is usually needed. Use the principle, not the number. Required frequency varies by price, category familiarity and channel.

## Core idea
One touch rarely converts a cold prospect. Plan a sequence of varied touchpoints across channels so the brand becomes familiar and objections get answered over time.

## E-com application
Build touchpoint maps rather than single campaigns:

| Stage | Touchpoint examples |
|---|---|
| Discover | Organic social, SEO article, creator mention, PR |
| Consider | Retargeting ad, comparison content, review highlights |
| Capture | Email/SMS sign-up with lead magnet or quiz |
| Nurture | Welcome series, education emails, UGC, FAQ answers |
| Convert | Offer, abandonment reminders, social proof |
| Re-engage | Post-purchase, replenishment, win-back |

## When to use
- Planning email/SMS flows and retargeting
- Setting expectations for paid social (first-touch ROAS will undercount)
- Attribution debates ("this channel doesn't convert" may mean it opens the sequence)

## Diagnostic questions
1. How many touchpoints does a typical buyer have before first purchase? (Check analytics paths, post-purchase survey.)
2. Are touches varied (format, channel, message) or the same ad seven times?
3. Does each touch answer a different objection?
4. What frequency cap stops annoyance?

## Common misuse
- Treating it as "show the same ad 7 times".
- Ignoring creative fatigue and frequency caps.
- Using it to justify spend with no measurement.

## Pairs well with
TPB (each touch builds attitude, norms or control), Zeigarnik (reminders), RACE (touches across all stages), Racecar Fuel (content).
