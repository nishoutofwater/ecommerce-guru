# STP: Segmentation, Targeting, Positioning

**Origin:** Core strategic marketing model, popularised by Philip Kotler.

## Core idea
Decide who you serve before deciding what you say or sell.

### 1. Segmentation
Divide the market by meaningful differences.
| Basis | E-com examples |
|---|---|
| Demographic | Age, income, life stage |
| Geographic | Country, shipping zone, climate, urban/rural |
| Psychographic | Values, lifestyle, identity |
| Behavioural | Purchase frequency, AOV, channel, first-time vs repeat, price sensitivity |
| Needs/jobs | The job the product does ("recover faster", "gift for a colleague") |

### 2. Targeting
Choose segments to prioritise. Score on: size, growth, reachability (can we target them on allowed channels?), margin, competitive intensity, fit with our product.

### 3. Positioning
Define the place you want in the target's mind.

Template:
> For **[target segment]** who **[need]**, **[brand]** is the **[category]** that **[key benefit]** because **[reason to believe]**. Unlike **[alternative]**, we **[differentiator]**.

## When to use
- Always first, before 4Ps, messaging, or channel plans
- Expanding to new markets or categories
- When ads, copy and product feel scattered

## Diagnostic questions
1. Which customers have the highest LTV and lowest CAC today? Who are they?
2. Can we reach this segment on channels open to our category?
3. Is our positioning a claim a competitor could also make? If yes, sharpen.
4. Do we have proof (reason to believe) for the claim?

## Common misuse
- Segmenting on demographics only when behaviour or needs explain purchase better.
- Targeting "everyone interested in X".
- Positioning statements with unprovable claims.

## Pairs well with
4Ps (STP then mix), Brand Resonance (meaning for the target), TPB (whose norms), Racecar (which kickstarts reach the target), RACE Plan stage.
