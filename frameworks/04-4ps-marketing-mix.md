# 4Ps of Marketing (Marketing Mix)

**Origin:** E. Jerome McCarthy (1960), building on Neil Borden's marketing mix. Extended versions add People, Process and Physical evidence (7Ps), which are useful for service-heavy stores.

## Core idea
Four controllable decisions that together define the offer. They must be consistent with each other and with the chosen segment and positioning.

| P | E-com decisions |
|---|---|
| **Product** | Range and assortment, hero SKUs, bundles, variants, packaging, unboxing, quality, subscriptions, guarantees |
| **Price** | Price architecture, anchors, tiers, bundles, discounts policy, free shipping threshold, payment plans, subscription pricing |
| **Place** | Own site, marketplaces (Amazon etc.), social commerce, retail/wholesale, geographies, fulfilment speed and coverage |
| **Promotion** | Channels (SEO, paid, email/SMS, influencers, affiliates, PR), messaging, offers, content |

Optional extensions: **People** (support quality), **Process** (checkout, delivery, returns), **Physical evidence** (packaging, site design, reviews).

## When to use
- Building or reviewing a full go-to-market for a product or store
- Checking consistency (a premium product with discount-heavy promotion is a mismatch)
- Launching into a new market or channel

## Diagnostic questions
1. Product: which SKUs drive 80% of revenue? Which drive acquisition vs margin?
2. Price: what is the reference price in the shopper's mind? Is our discounting training customers to wait?
3. Place: where does the target segment already buy? Are fulfilment times competitive in each market?
4. Promotion: which channels are allowed and effective for this category? (Some categories face ad platform restrictions.)
5. Do all four Ps tell the same story?

## Common misuse
- Treating each P separately, by different owners, with no shared positioning.
- Making Promotion the whole strategy.

## Pairs well with
STP (always do STP first), Decoy (price architecture), Brand Resonance (performance and imagery), Racecar (Promotion choice = growth engine).
